          How we get the number

          All your money sits in one portfolio. A dynamic program works backward from your last goal and, for every year and every possible balance, decides two things: which goals due that year to pay for (in full, the smaller version, or not at all), and which risk level, up to your limit, to hold for the year ahead. It picks whatever reaches the most of what you care about from then on, so whether a goal gets paid depends on how much money is actually there when it comes due. In each simulated future, the portfolio grows or shrinks by a random year in the market, using Wₓ₊₁ = Wₓ · exp[(µ − ½σ²) + σZ], at the level the plan chose for that balance. Your probability is the share of futures in which the goal gets paid. Each point of importance counts about 1.4 times as much as the one below it, so your top goals come first. The method follows Das, Ostrov, Radhakrishnan and Srivastav, Dynamic Optimization for Multi-Goals Wealth Management.
