# Usability Test: Interview Script

We run two usability tests on the working app, in pairs. Every member is an
interviewer at least once, and nobody leads more than one test.

## Before we start

- Go through the consent form first. The participant and both interviewers
  sign it; the signed form is part of the submission.
- Ask before recording. Recording is optional, and the participant can say no.
- The lead asks the questions. The note-taker writes down what the participant
  says and does: where they hesitate, wrong clicks, quotes, and roughly how
  long each task takes.

## Intro (read aloud)

> Thanks for helping us. We're testing the app, not you, so there are no wrong
> answers, and anything that's confusing is our problem to fix. As you go,
> please think out loud: tell us what you're looking at, what you expect to
> happen, and what you're unsure about. During the tasks we might not answer
> questions right away, because we want to see how the app works on its own.
> You can stop at any time.

## Part A: How you handle it today (about 5 minutes)

1. What are you saving for right now? Walk me through how you keep track of it.
2. Tell me about a time you had to choose between two things you were saving
   for. How did you decide?
3. How do you currently figure out whether you'll have enough by the time you
   need it?
4. Tell me about a time a savings plan didn't go the way you expected. What did
   you do next?

## Part B: Tasks (think aloud)

Read each task as written. Don't name buttons, steps or labels.

Scenario: *You take home about $4,800 a month and spend about $3,500. You have
about $12,000 in savings, and you want to keep $4,000 of it for emergencies.*

1. Enter your money and find out how much you have left each month.
2. Yesterday you spent $86 on dinner. Record it.
   - Follow-up: What is this part of the page telling you?
3. You want to go to Japan next September, which will cost about $3,000. You
   also want a $40,000 house down payment by June 2030, and you'd settle for
   $30,000 if you had to. The house matters more to you. Add both goals.
4. Find out how likely you are to reach each goal.
   - Follow-up: In your own words, what is this page telling you?
5. The trip now costs $3,800. Update it.
6. You're thinking about putting an extra $200 a month toward your goals. Find
   out what that would change.
7. You've decided to skip the trip. Remove it.
8. *(If built by test day)* Choose how much investment risk you're comfortable
   with, and compare the options.
9. *(If built by test day)* Find out whether your retirement savings would last.

### After each task

- What did you expect to happen?
- Was there anything you weren't sure about?
- How confident are you in that number? What makes you say that?

### If the participant gets stuck

- What are you looking for?
- What would you try next?
- Don't point to the answer. If they're stuck for more than about two minutes,
  note it and move on.

## Part C: Wrap-up

1. Which part was hardest? Why?
2. Was there anything you expected to see that wasn't there?
3. How does this compare to the way you plan your savings today?
4. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
5. Is there anything else you'd like to tell us?

## Phrasing to avoid

Leading questions suggest an answer. Ask openly instead:

| Avoid | Ask instead |
|---|---|
| "Was that easy?" | "How did that go?" |
| "Is the chart helpful?" | "What does the chart tell you?" |
| "Would you use this?" | "When, if ever, would you use something like this?" |
| "Click the Chances tab." | "Find out how likely you are to reach each goal." |

## After the interview

- Each interviewer writes their own notes the same day.
- Combine them into one processed-notes document.
- Write one summary paragraph: what we learned, with examples, and whether it
  leads to changes in the app and why or why not.
